## Introduction

This report presents an analysis of patient appointment data for the HealthConnect Experience Lab. The objective of the analysis was to evaluate appointment attendance, cancellations, and no-shows while identifying factors that influence patient behavior. Microsoft Power BI was used to clean, transform, analyze, and visualize the dataset through an interactive dashboard.
The dashboard focuses on key performance indicators (KPIs), appointment outcomes, demographic analysis, booking behavior, reminder effectiveness, previous appointment effect on appointment outcome, clinic accessibility and appointment trends over time. The findings provide valuable insights that can help healthcare administrators improve patient attendance, reduce missed appointments, and enhance operational efficiency.


## Business Objectives

The analysis was designed to answer the following business questions:
- Which appointment type has the highest and lowest attendance rate?
- Does attendance vary by gender?
- Does attendance vary by age group?
- What is the trend in appointment volume over time?
- How does booking lead time correlate with no-shows and cancellation?
- How does distance to the clinic correlate with attendance?
- What recommendation should be asserted to the hospital management?


 ## Dashboard KPIs

The dashboard highlights the following overall metrics:
- Total Appointments: 5,000
- Attendance Rate: 46.28%
- Cancellation Rate: 5.26%
- No-Show Rate: 48.46%
- Average Booking Lead Time: 29.64 days
- Average Distance to Clinic: 10.08 km

These KPIs provide a quick overview of appointment performance and patient behavior.


## Key Findings

### Question 1: Which appointment type has the highest and lowest attendance rate?

The analysis shows that less than half of all scheduled appointments were attended, while almost half resulted in no-shows. Cancellation rates remained relatively low compared to no-shows, indicating that patients are more likely to miss appointments without formally cancelling them.

Attendance varied slightly across appointment types.
General consultations recorded the highest attendance rate, while follow-up appointments experienced the highest no-show rate. Specialist consultations and diagnostic tests performed similarly, with attendance and no-show rates remaining close.
This suggests that patients may place greater priority on initial consultations than follow-up appointments.


### Question 2: Does attendance vary by gender?

Attendance patterns were similar across male and female patients. No significant difference was observed between genders, suggesting that gender alone is not a major determinant of appointment attendance.


### Question 3: Does attendance vary by age group?

Older patients demonstrated better attendance compared to younger patients.
Patients aged 65 years and above recorded the highest number of attended appointments, while younger adults experienced comparatively higher no-show rates.
This indicates that age may influence commitment to scheduled appointments.



### Question 4: What is the trend in appointment volume over time?

Monthly analysis shows fluctuations in attendance, cancellations, and no-shows throughout the year.
Attendance remained relatively stable across most months, while cancellations stayed consistently low. No-show counts fluctuated but remained high throughout the reporting period, indicating an ongoing operational challenge rather than isolated incidents.


### Question 5: How does booking lead time correlate with no-shows and cancellation?

The relationship between booking lead time and appointment outcome shows that patients who booked appointments several weeks in advance experienced higher numbers of no-shows than those with shorter booking lead times.
This suggests that longer waiting periods between booking and appointment dates may increase the likelihood of patients forgetting or abandoning their appointments.


### Question 6: How does distance to the clinic correlate with attendance?

The analysis indicates that most appointments occurred among patients living relatively close to the clinic.
Attendance gradually declined as travel distance increased. Although the number of appointments also decreased for patients living farther away, greater distances appear to contribute to lower attendance.
This finding suggests that clinic accessibility may influence appointment completion.


## Additional Findings

Reminder analysis produced several important findings.
SMS was the most frequently used reminder channel, followed by no reminder, WhatsApp, and Email.
Patients who received reminders generally recorded higher attendance than those who did not receive reminders. However, reminders alone did not eliminate no-shows, suggesting that additional interventions may be required.
The comparison of reminder channels indicates slight differences in attendance performance, although SMS appears to be the most widely utilized communication method.

The majority of patients had 0 previous appointments, and this group recorded the highest number of attended appointments. As the number of previous appointments increased, the total number of appointments decreased across all outcomes (attended, cancelled, and no-show). However, among patients with multiple previous appointments, no-shows remained relatively high compared to cancellations. This suggests that most patients are either new patients or have limited appointment history. Patients with repeated appointments continue to miss appointments at a noticeable rate, indicating that frequent users of the healthcare service may require additional engagement to improve attendance.

Patients with no previous no-shows accounted for the largest number of appointments and also had the highest attendance. However, patients with one or more previous no-shows continued to record a significant number of current no-shows. Although the number of patients with multiple previous no-shows is relatively small, they consistently show poor attendance behavior.

This indicates that previous no-show history is a strong indicator of future appointment behavior. Patients who have missed appointments before are more likely to miss future appointments, making previous no-shows an important predictor of attendance.



## Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:
- Strengthen Reminder Systems
Continue using SMS reminders while increasing reminder frequency closer to appointment dates. Multiple reminders may further reduce missed appointments.
- Reduce Long Booking Lead Times
Where possible, shorten the waiting period between booking and appointment dates to minimize forgotten appointments and scheduling conflicts.
- Improve Follow-up Appointment Engagement
Since follow-up appointments recorded the highest no-show rate, implement targeted reminder campaigns and patient education to encourage attendance.
- Support Patients Living Far from the Clinic
Patients travelling longer distances may benefit from appointment flexibility, transport assistance, or telemedicine services where appropriate.
- Target Younger Patients
Younger patients demonstrated comparatively lower attendance. Tailored communication strategies and digital reminders could improve appointment adherence within this age group.
- Monitor Reminder Channel Performance
Continue evaluating the effectiveness of SMS, Email, and WhatsApp reminders to determine the most successful communication method.
- The clinic should identify patients with a history of previous no-shows and implement targeted interventions, such as:

Additional SMS or phone call reminders.

Appointment confirmation 24–48 hours before the visit.
Easier rescheduling options.

Flagging high-risk patients for follow-up by administrative staff.
These measures could help reduce no-show rates, improve clinic efficiency, and ensure appointment slots are used more effectively.


## Conclusion

The HealthConnect Experience Lab dashboard provides valuable insights into patient appointment behavior. The analysis reveals that no-shows represent the greatest operational challenge, accounting for almost half of scheduled appointments. Age, booking lead time, appointment type, reminder strategy, and clinic distance all demonstrate varying degrees of influence on attendance outcomes.

By implementing stronger reminder systems, reducing booking lead times, improving follow-up appointment management, and supporting patients facing accessibility challenges, healthcare administrators can improve attendance rates, reduce missed appointments, and optimize clinic operations.
